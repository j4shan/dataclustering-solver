OUTPUT
One static PNG, 1920 x 1080, warm off-white ground, single light palette.

SUBJECT
A pharmacist filling a batch of prescriptions from a dispensary rack. The figure
teaches one mechanism in ordinary language: the chart names a drawer, the whole
drawer comes out, and everything that shared that drawer is handled and put back.
It is the first illustration of Section A, so it stays entirely inside the
dispensary. It carries no database vocabulary, no symbols, no formulas, no code
and no technical mapping labels -- earning those names is the next figure's job.

The rack is the centre of the drawing. It must read as a real dispensary
fixture: a tall cabinet carcass with rows of small, identical drawers set into
it, each drawer holding a handful of paper cartons and glass bottles.

STYLE
Editorial flat 2D diagram, horizontal flow, restrained dispensary palette. No
perspective, realism, 3D, shadows, gradients, UI chrome, or emojis. Drawn as a
straight-on elevation -- the viewer faces the rack squarely.
System-ui labels; ui-monospace for drawer numbers and the counter readout.
Rounded corners ~8px on drawers, cartons and slips, ~10px on the rack carcass
and the counter panels. Crisp 2px arrows.

COLOURS
This figure shares one palette with the two problem-statement figures, slot for
slot, so all three read as one family:
#FCFCFB canvas . #7A1F2B wine-red (opened drawer stroke, wanted item, arrows) .
#F8EEF0 warm-rose (opened drawer fill, hatch ground) . #1B4D3E dark-green (shut
drawer stroke dashed, shut drawer type and contents) . #EEF4F0 cool-sage (shut
drawer fill) . #0B0B0B ink . #52514E secondary . #DEDEDE rules . #F3F3F3 chart
band . #FFFFFF counter and rack ground.
Do not use orange. Do not introduce a fifth hue for the bottles or the cartons --
they are told apart by shape, never by colour.

THE INSTANCE (fixed -- do not vary)
A rack of 9 small drawers, 3 rows of 3, numbered 1-9 in reading order. Every
drawer holds exactly 4 items, so the rack holds 36 items in all, and the drawer
capacity is the same everywhere and never changes in this figure.

The counter batch is three prescriptions waiting together:
    Rx 1 -> cough syrup       Rx 2 -> eye drops       Rx 3 -> antacid

The drawer chart names three drawers -- 3, 6 and 9, the rightmost drawer of each
row, so each one slides clear of the rack's right edge without colliding with a
neighbour. Those three are drawn OPEN. The other six stay shut.

Drawer contents, in order, with the packaging each item is drawn as:

  1  plasters (carton) . gauze pads (carton) . adhesive tape (carton) .

  2  vitamin C (bottle) . vitamin D (bottle) . iron tablets (bottle) .
     zinc tablets (bottle)
  3  cough syrup (bottle) [WANTED] . throat lozenges (carton) .
     chest rub (carton) . steam capsules (carton)
  4  nasal spray (bottle) . sinus rinse (bottle) . decongestant tablets

  5  hand cream (carton) . lip balm (carton) . sunscreen (carton) .
     aloe gel (bottle)
  6  eye drops (bottle) [WANTED] . ear drops (bottle) . eye wash (bottle) .
     lens solution (bottle)
  7  pain relief tablets (carton) . muscle gel (carton) . heat patches

  8  rehydration salts (carton) . anti-nausea tablets (carton) . motion bands

Therefore: 3 drawers opened, 6 drawers shut, 12 items on the counter, 3 items
kept, 9 items put back, 24 items never touched. The wasted effort is three times
the useful effort, and the drawing must make that ratio visible at a glance, not
only in the readout.

CUTAWAY CONVENTION
The rack is drawn as a cutaway: the contents of the shut drawers are visible to
the reader even though the pharmacist never sees them. The shut drawers keep
their dashed border and their idle treatment throughout, which is what stops
them reading as rummaged.

THE ITEM GLYPHS
Two silhouettes, and only two. The shape says what the packaging is; it never
says anything about whether an item was wanted:
  CARTON  a plain rounded rectangle, wider than tall, with one horizontal fold
          line across the upper third and a short vertical seam at the right
          edge -- a folded paper medicine box seen face on.
  BOTTLE  a rounded rectangle with a narrower neck and a flat cap on top, and a
          single label band across its middle -- a glass dispensary bottle.
Both glyphs are the same overall height so a drawer's row of four sits on one
baseline. Draw them at ~34 x 26 inside the rack, ~104 x 74 on the counter.

KEEP OUT
- Technical vocabulary appears ONLY in the parenthetical of the four section
  titles. Every other label, tag, note and legend entry stays in dispensary
  language -- no data, warehouse, record, materialized, selected, layout,
  assignment or strategy anywhere else in the image.
- No patient names, ages, dosages, strengths, instructions, or real brand names.
  The items are ordinary pharmacy stock and nothing reads as medical advice.
- Only two item silhouettes, the carton and the bottle. No pill, capsule,
  syringe, cross or caduceus pictograms.
- No arrow originating at a single item, slip or chart row, and no arrow that
  reaches, wraps or passes a shut drawer.
- No distinction carried by colour alone.

PRODUCTION
Every label must clear its element; the chart's two columns must not collide;
the opened drawers must overlap the carcass edge without touching the counter;
and the lower counter panel must stay on canvas -- fail the render rather than
clip.
