OUTPUT
One static PNG, 1920 x 1080, warm off-white ground, single light palette.

SUBJECT
A naive smart organizer rearranging a dispensary's drawers from demand history.
The figure teaches one mechanism: count how often each item is pulled, average
those counts, cut the range at two thresholds, and refill fixed-capacity drawers
band by band. It is deliberately the simplest rule that could work -- rank, cut
twice, fill in order -- and the figure says so rather than implying cleverness.

STYLE
Editorial flat 2D diagram, two components side by side. No perspective, realism,
3D, shadows, gradients, UI chrome, or emojis. System-ui labels; ui-monospace for
every count, average and drawer number. Rounded corners ~8px on drawers and
rows, ~10px on cards. 1px rules.

COLOURS
Shares one palette with the other figures in this family, slot for slot:
#FCFCFB canvas . #7A1F2B wine-red (demand bars, high band, thresholds) .
#F8EEF0 warm-rose (high-band fill) . #1B4D3E dark-green (low band) . #EEF4F0
cool-sage (low-band fill) . #0B0B0B ink . #52514E secondary . #DEDEDE rules .
#F3F3F3 header band . #FFFFFF card ground.
Do not use orange. The middle band takes no hue of its own -- it is neutral ink
on white, which is what makes it read as the band between the two.

THE INSTANCE (fixed -- do not vary)
The same 36 items, in the same packaging, as the retrieval figure. Each carries
three usage counts, one per sampled hour. Every count is a non-negative integer:
the number of times that item was pulled from inventory in that hour.

The two thresholds are DERIVED, never typed: sort the 36 items by average hourly
count, split into three equal groups of twelve, and take the lowest average in
each of the upper two groups. For this instance that yields T1 = 2.0 /hr and
T2 = 6.0 /hr, and the stated rule reproduces the grouping exactly:
    avg < T1              -> LOW      (12 items)
    T1 <= avg < T2        -> MEDIUM   (12 items)
    avg >= T2             -> HIGH     (12 items)
Drawer capacity is 4, so each band fills exactly three drawers and the cabinet
holds nine, as before. Drawers are numbered 1-9, low band first.

COMPONENT 1 -- USAGE REPORT (left)
White card, 1px #DEDEDE border, rounded 10px, #F3F3F3 header strip reading

Columns, left to right:
    item_name | used_hour_x | used_hour_y | used_hour_z | avg /hr
The three used_hour columns are the stored data, set in mono and right-aligned.
The avg /hr column is DERIVED, so it is set apart: mono Semibold ink on a faint
#F3F3F3 column ground, with its header italic-weighted to mark it as computed.
Thirty-six rows, sorted by avg /hr ASCENDING, set in two blocks of eighteen
side by side. Rows are separated by 1px #DEDEDE rules.

The two thresholds are drawn ON the table, where the sort actually cuts: a 2px
solid wine rule across the block, marked with a narrow canvas-coloured chip
reading only "T1" or "T2". The chip carries NO value -- the strip below and the
band headers do -- and it is parked in the gutter between the item names and the
counts, so it never sits on a row's data. T1 falls after the twelfth row (first
block), T2 after the twenty-fourth (second block, sixth row). These two rules are
the only wine in the table.

Beneath the card, 13.5px secondary:

   avg /hr is derived, not stored"

SPLIT RULE STRIP (under the usage report)
A single horizontal bar, 16px tall, rounded 8px, spanning the card's width and
divided into three segments whose widths are proportional to the value ranges
they cover, so the strip is a true number line from 0 to the highest average:

Tick labels sit beneath the boundaries in mono 11.5: 0, T1, T2 and the maximum.
Band names sit above their segment in tracked 11.5px Semibold, in the band's own
ink. This strip is the only place the two thresholds appear as a picture.

A drawer never mixes bands, and no drawer is left part-filled: 12 items divided

  "capacity is fixed at 4, so each band fills exactly 3 drawers"

KEEP OUT
- No patient names, ages, dosages, strengths, instructions, or real brand names.
  The counts are inventory pulls and nothing reads as medical advice.
- Only two item silhouettes, the carton and the bottle. No pill, capsule,
  syringe, cross or caduceus pictograms.
- No claim that this layout is optimal, recommended, or the best available. The
  figure demonstrates one naive rule and says that it is naive.
- No second scenario, no before-and-after pair, no retrieval walkthrough -- the
  previous figure owns that, and repeating it here would make two things to keep
  true.
- No distinction carried by colour alone.

PRODUCTION
The two components share one top edge and one bottom edge. Every threshold and
every count in the figure is computed from the instance, never written as a
literal. Column heads must clear their data, the demand bar track must not
collide with the item name at its longest, and the legend must stay on one row
or wrap cleanly -- fail the render rather than clip.
