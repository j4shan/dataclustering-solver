SUBJECT
The container assignment problem on an already-chosen logical split.
A price-tier CASE cuts Original Data into two terminal groups. A shared
store of 1,000 buckets can send containers to those groups. The objective
is to raise the weighted-average Poisson data-skipping ratio without
exceeding that fixed budget. It is the illustration of Drawing Storage
Boundary §1. It is not an allocator, knapsack ladder, greedy ranking, or
recommendation.

groups = {

A column is named once, in `column`, and every reference to it is

Two arms, and only two. The bounds tile the price line: the first arm is
open above $20, the second closes at $0, and they meet at $20. The CASE
ladder and the edge ranges are both read off those bounds, so the figure
cannot show a ladder that disagrees with the ranges beside it.

The two groups are a hypothesized price-tier cut, not a partition of a
provider corpus or a catalog score. States and metrics on this figure
are hypothesized and then derived from the dictionary above. n1, n2,
K1, K2, pg1, pg2, F1 and F2 are designated symbols, not numeric values.
The record counts in the dictionary fix the instance and are checked by
the renderer; no record count is printed in the figure.

DERIVATION (the renderer computes all of it)
  N = sum(records)                              # 3,800,000
  w_g = n_g / N                                 # population weight
  ladder(groups)   -> the CASE string, from the bounds
  range_tag(g)     -> "[Price] > $20" for the open arm,
                      "[Price] <= $20" for the arm that closes at $0
  F_i(K_i) = e^(-n_i * pg_i / K_i)              # the expected data
                                                # skipping ratio: the
                                                # Poisson empty-unit
                                                # probability, in (0, 1]
  V_i(K_i) = n_i * F_i(K_i)                     # skipping volume, in
                                                # records
  marginal(g) = delta(n_i F_i) / delta K_i      # change in skipping
                                                # volume per container

Each leaf owns one n_i, one K_i, one pg_i and one F_i from the
dictionary. The expected data skipping ratio on that leaf is the function
F_i of its own container count, F_i(K_i) = e^(-n_i pg_i / K_i). Naming it
as a function is what lets the marginal row be written as a change in
volume rather than as a second exponential. This figure does not bind
n_i, K_i, pg_i or F_i to a number.

F_i carries no population weight. It is a per-group empty-unit
probability, defined inside the group. The weight w_g enters one level up,
in the weighted average the objective states.

The marginal benefit is the change in the leaf's skipping volume per
container, written delta(n_i * F_i) / delta K_i with the delta glyph. The
volume is spelled out as n_i * F_i so the record count is visibly inside
the differentiated quantity. Every product prints its multiplication
sign -- in the exponent and in the volume alike; no product is written by
juxtaposition alone. Do not print a step size, an accepted
upgrade, or a ranking of these values across leaves.

Expected derived checks:
  N = 3,800,000
  w(T1) = 500,000 / 3,800,000
  w(T0) = 3,300,000 / 3,800,000
  K1 + K2 = 1,000
  T1 -> (n1, K1, pg1, F1) . T0 -> (n2, K2, pg2, F2)
  the arms meet at $20 and close at $0

The renderer asserts N, the two weights, the two symbol quadruples, and
the tiling of the bounds. It never reads these check values as source
data.
